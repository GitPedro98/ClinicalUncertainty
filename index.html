SUS-UncertaintyBench

A pre-pilot leaderboard for frontier language models on Brazilian emergency medicine, under controlled divergence between training and deployment context.

85 physician-scored responses on 30 items from 10 clinical archetypes, three models at temperature 0. Single annotator. September 2026.

At this sample size, the three models cannot be ranked.

Across the 25 items answered by all three models, a Friedman test finds no difference in scores (χ² = 0.83, p = 0.66). Every 95% interval overlaps the others. GLM 5.2 places first in 67% of bootstrap resamples and Kimi K2.7 Code in 32%, so the order below is a point estimate, not a ranking.

How often each model lands in each place

Resampling archetypes 10,000 times and re-ranking each time shows how much of the order is noise. DeepSeek V4 Pro almost never places first, but no model is reliably ahead of the others.

The benchmark measures something stable

The strongest result is about the instrument, not the models. Items that are hard for one model are hard for the others: Kendall’s coefficient of concordance across the three models is W = 0.58 (p = 0.014), with pairwise rank correlations between 0.61 and 0.71. Independently trained models agree on which cases are difficult.

A mixed-effects model with a random intercept per archetype attributes 36% of score variance to the case itself. Model identity is not a significant term.

Where the difficulty sits

The two ends of the archetype ranking are both coronary cases. CARDIO_03, an acute inferior STEMI with every finding produced at admission, scored 2.00 across all eight responses. CARDIO_01, an asymptomatic follow-up in which the ECG, troponin and echocardiogram were all two months old, scored 0.22 with no passing response. The difference between them is whether the model has to place the evidence in time before reading it.

By dimension, local constraints in cardiovascular cases sit furthest from the neurological cases, and their intervals do not overlap. Within cardiology, however, the three dimensions cannot be told apart: the pre-pilot does not support a claim that any one dimension is the weakest.

How robust is the category difference

Treating the archetype as the unit, neurological cases score 0.62 higher. An exact permutation test over all 252 ways of splitting ten archetypes into two groups of five gives p = 0.048. The result depends on one case: without CARDIO_01 the difference falls to 0.38 (p = 0.087).

Response to perturbation

For context-blindness items, each second prompt changed one variable that should change the plan. Responses that kept their initial plan failed outright more often than those that revised it, 6 of 28 against 3 of 30 (odds ratio 2.45). The direction is consistent with rigidity rather than oscillation, but it is not significant (Fisher p = 0.29). Whether a response revised its plan was classified automatically and checked against three annotator notes.

Adjusted estimates

Fixed effects from the mixed model, with GLM 5.2, cardiovascular and temporal reasoning as references. Only the category term excludes zero.

What sample size would settle these questions

The pre-pilot is underpowered for both comparisons, by design. Simulating from the fitted variance components, detecting the observed category difference with 80% power needs about ten archetypes per category; the pre-pilot has five. Separating models that differ by the observed 0.24 needs roughly 70 paired items; it has 25. The proposed next phase, 30 archetypes and about 180 variations, reaches 80% power for both.

Method and limits

Each archetype holds a clinical core fixed and varies one controlled variable between a first and a second prompt. Responses were scored pass, partial or fail against conditions written before inference, by one emergency physician. Of 172 collected pairs, the 85 testing context blindness and relational reasoning are scored; prior override and reasoning spectrum are not yet.

The largest limitation is the single annotator: there is no inter-rater agreement yet. All perturbations are directional, so unjustified oscillation cannot be measured. The three models were chosen for API cost, not as a sample of the frontier. Calibration metrics described in the protocol need confidence elicitation that this version of the prompts does not include.

Intervals come from resampling archetypes, the level at which responses are correlated. With ten archetypes, interval coverage is approximate.
